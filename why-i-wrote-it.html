Why I wrote…

The Great Reveal

Though it takes place in the near future, The Great Reveal is my testament to recent times—a way to acknowledge the tragedy and absurdity, commiserate through humor, and remind all of us, hang in there. I wanted to explore the cognitive tug of war between the need and responsibility to be vigilant and take a stand (and to the streets) against fascism and other social ills versus the desire, and even obligation (by virtue of privilege), to enjoy life, and the need to stay sane by tuning out current events. I thought of Geneva—as birthplace of the League of Nations, a magnet for billionaires, host to peace talks, and a microcosm of a country where the right wing is gaining over the Green movement—as an ideal setting for a story about confronting climate change, autocracy, and AI. The narrative highlights themes of freedom and, as was the case with Seeing Green, of belonging and intergenerational unity.

Seeing Green

Seeing Green was my (belated) response to Primary Colors—a version of campaigning that’s women-driven, international, and includes broader themes around public interest work and identity. I wanted to show an activist’s multidimensionality and to focus on the lives of underdogs who have linkages to power but are relatively powerless. Told in the first person, the book captures early-90s Washington and the nuts and bolts of its politics with coming-of-age intensity. Though some of that era’s public debates and policies have been remarkably cyclical, it was nevertheless a historically unique time of optimism in the US that shaped several generations.
